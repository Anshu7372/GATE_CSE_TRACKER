Which of the following are valid rules of inference?
(A) From p and p → q, infer q.
(B) From ¬q and p → q, infer ¬p.
(C) From q and p → q, infer p.
(D) From p ∨ q and ¬p, infer q.
(E) From p → q, infer q → p.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
o:
A. (A), (B) and (C) Only
B. (A), (B) and (D) Only
C. (B), (D) and (E) Only
D. (A), (C), (D) and (E) Only
ans: B
== hint
(A) modus ponens, (B) modus tollens, (D) disjunctive syllogism. (C) "affirming the consequent" aur (E) converse — fallacies.
== concept
### Rules of inference
- **Modus ponens**: p, p → q ∴ q.
- **Modus tollens**: ¬q, p → q ∴ ¬p.
- **Hypothetical syllogism**: p → q, q → r ∴ p → r.
- **Disjunctive syllogism**: p ∨ q, ¬p ∴ q.
- **Addition**: p ∴ p ∨ q. **Simplification**: p ∧ q ∴ p.
- **Resolution**: p ∨ q, ¬p ∨ r ∴ q ∨ r.
### Fallacies
- Affirming the consequent: q, p → q ∴ p ✗.
- Denying the antecedent: ¬p, p → q ∴ ¬q ✗.
- Converse/inverse ko original ke barabar maanna ✗.
== tips
- Validity check: premises sab T aur conclusion F ho sake → invalid.
== sol
(A) ✓, (B) ✓, (C) ✗ (p = F, q = T counter-example), (D) ✓, (E) ✗.

**Answer: (B) (A), (B) and (D) Only**
== refs
- Kenneth Rosen — Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications, Sec 1.6 (Rules of inference, fallacies).
